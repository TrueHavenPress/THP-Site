AMAZON OPTIMIZATION

If we published your book within the last three months, this is already done. Your categories, keywords, and metadata were optimized during production, and you do not need this service. This page is for authors we did not publish, and for authors we did publish more than three months ago whose listings are due for a refresh.

Online book listings age quietly, but not gracefully. Amazon reorganizes its ranking categories, some of them disappear, and the search terms readers type shift as buying behavior shifts. A book that was well positioned two years ago can now be competing in categories that no longer carry traffic, against keywords no one is searching. Nothing on the listing looks broken, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed.

The clearest example is still working its way through Amazon’s catalog. Until 2023, authors chose BISAC codes and could email KDP support to request up to ten additional category placements. That system is gone. Today you select up to three store categories per format, directly, and support will not add more. Many books published before the change still show their old placements, but most of those category paths no longer exist as options in KDP, so they do little to put the book in front of readers.

What You Receive

  • Category and keyword research, current as of today. We re-research your BISAC subject codes, Amazon browse and ranking categories, and backend keywords against current traffic and current competition, not against what worked when the book launched. Categories are chosen for winnability, not for size, and we go as deep into the subcategory tree as your book can honestly claim.
  • A rewritten book description. Most descriptions are written once, during a launch week, by an exhausted author who has stopped being able to see the book from the outside. They summarize the plot instead of giving a reader a reason to buy. We rewrite yours as sales copy, with the hook in the opening lines where it still shows above the fold, and formatted so it reads cleanly rather than as a wall of text.
  • An Author Central refresh. Author Central is a second storefront most authors set up once and never return to. It carries your bio, your photo, and your full catalog, and it appears next to every book you publish. We rewrite the bio to position you rather than merely list you, confirm every title is claimed and correctly linked to your author name, and make sure the page presents consistently with your listing.
  • Metadata updates across formats. Each format of your book gets its own category slots. eBook, paperback, and hardcover are handled as the separate listings Amazon treats them as, rather than optimized once and assumed to carry.
  • What we review: BISAC subject codes, Amazon browse and ranking categories, backend keywords, book description and metadata, Author Central setup, and category placement against current competition.
  • Includes: Up to 3 book versions—eBook, paperback, hardback—for one title. Deeply discounted bulk pricing available for multiple standalone or serial titles.

How It Works

  • We Research and Rewrite. Categories, keywords, description, and bio are researched and drafted against what Amazon shows readers today. You see every recommendation before anything goes live.
  • You Review. Ask questions, request revisions, collaborate with us. You know your book better than anyone else, and we know how to help potential buyers find and choose it. Two rounds of revisions are included, and together we finalize the content and decisions that make your listing stronger.
  • We Update Your Listing. We coordinate a time with you to log into your KDP, Author Central, and IngramSpark accounts to make the updates for you.

What It Does Not Do

This service improves where your book can be found. It does not guarantee rank, sales, or placement. Amazon reserves the right to reassign a book it judges miscategorized, and no one selling optimization can promise otherwise. It also does not change your cover, your interior, or your price, and it cannot add categories beyond the three per format Amazon currently allows.

Strategy Note

Order matters here. A bestseller campaign drives rank into whichever categories your book currently occupies, so optimizing first means the campaign pushes your book up a shelf readers actually browse rather than one that no longer carries traffic. The same logic applies to Amazon Ads and to any social or press push: traffic arriving at a stale listing converts worse than traffic arriving at a current one. Worth repeating annually for the same reason it was worth doing the first time. The ground moves whether or not the book does.
