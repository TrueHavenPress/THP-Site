# True Haven Press — Frequently Asked Questions

## Page introduction

Publishing decisions deserve straight answers, so these are written plainly and without hedging. If your question is not here, call 1-800-585-3690 or book a consultation so we can answer it directly.

## Section 1 — Manuscript submission and fit

### What genres do you publish?

**We publish across most fiction and nonfiction—memoir, literary and commercial fiction, self-help, and lead-magnet business books among them. The only category we decline outright is erotica. There is no minimum or maximum word count. What matters more than genre is whether the book is distinctive and has an audience you can reach.**

Because we do not build a margin into your royalties, we are not asking the question a traditional publisher has to ask, which is whether a book will move enough copies to justify a print run and a slot on a list. We are asking whether the content is unique and authentic, whether there is a defined readership, and whether there is a realistic path to reaching it.

The rest of what we evaluate is about the working relationship: coachability, commitment to the process, and a partnership mindset. Publishing is collaborative, and the books that do well are the ones where the author shows up to review, revise, and approve at every stage.

### Does my manuscript have to be finished?

**No. We accept completed manuscripts, partial drafts, and proposals. If your book is unfinished, send what exists—we need to read the actual pages to determine whether it needs developmental editing or ghostwriting support to reach completion. That determination cannot be made from a description of the book you intend to write.**

A partial draft tells us things a summary cannot: how you sound on the page, where the structure is holding, and where the writing stalled. Those are the same things that tell us what kind of help would actually move the book forward and whether that help is editorial or collaborative.

Send what you have and tell us what it is. Nothing gets judged as a finished book if you have told us it is not one.

### What percentage of submissions do you accept?

**About 40% of the manuscripts submitted are accepted.**

Our criteria differ from a traditional publisher's because our economics do. We take no percentage of your royalties, so we are not screening for books that will earn out an advance. We are screening for books with real potential and for authors we can work with productively to produce books we can collaboratively be proud of and that have a real chance for the author to achieve success.

A meaningful share of our declines have little to do with the quality of the writing. They come down to fit—a book that needs more work than the author is prepared to do, or a working relationship that would not serve either of us. When that is the reason, we say so.

That figure describes manuscripts submitted for consideration. Ghostwriting engagements work differently, because the evaluation happens before the writing rather than after it. What that path does not come with is an automatic imprint, and the ghostwriting answer below explains why.

### What happens if my manuscript is not accepted?

**You receive a manuscript assessment and a marketability report at no charge—a clear account of why we passed and what to focus on to make the book stronger. There is no submission fee and no assessment fee. You can revise and resubmit as many times as you like, also at no charge.**

Most publishers send a form rejection, and most authors learn nothing from it. That seems like a waste of a manuscript somebody spent years on. A decline from us tells you where the book is losing the reader, where the positioning is unclear, and what would have to change for the answer to be different.

A no from us is not a verdict on the book. It is a snapshot of where the book is right now, and plenty of authors have sent us a second version we said yes to.

### What if my book is already published?

**We take on previously published books, provided you own the rights. Authors come to us after a disappointing run with a vanity press, after rights revert from a traditional publisher, or with a self-published title that never received professional editing or design. A relaunch is a real publishing project, and we treat it as one.**

What we will need to know early: where the current edition is listed, who holds the production files, whether the existing edition needs to come down before a new one goes live, and whether your rights are documented if they reverted from a prior publisher.

A relaunched edition publishes with a new ISBN, since it is a new edition from a different publisher of record. Reviews and sales history attached to the old listing generally do not transfer, and any publisher who tells you otherwise is guessing.

For books published in the author’s KDP/IngramSpark accounts, it’s often possible to update those listings rather than take them down and start over, meaning reviews and sales history are not lost. Book a consultation call for us to help you know what would be possible and advised for your situation.

## Section 2 — Cost, rights, and royalties

### What does publishing with True Haven Press cost?

**Most projects run between $2,500 and $9,500, covering editing through publication and marketing. Editing and marketing services can also be purchased à la carte if you do not need the full path. You receive a detailed, all-inclusive quote in writing with your publishing proposal, before you sign anything. What we quote is what you pay.**

What moves the number:

-   Manuscript length, and what type(s) of editing and how much is needed in each editing round

-   Whether the book needs ghostwriting support to reach completion

-   Interior complexity—images, tables, footnotes, endnotes, indexes

-   Design scope, including cover concepts and any custom illustration

-   Scope of marketing services—not a one-size-fits-all plan, this is customized per book and per budget

For context, hybrid publishing packages across the industry commonly run from around $5,000 to $25,000, and full-service packages that bundle ghostwriting run considerably higher. We sit deliberately at the bottom of that range and below it. That is a decision about how we make money, not a statement about the quality of the work—the same editors, designers, and production standards apply at $2,500 as at $9,500.

You will never receive a quote from us with a deadline attached. If we are a good fit, that will still be true next week.

### Do I keep the rights to my book?

**Yes, all of them. You retain 100% of your rights: print, eBook, audio, translation, film, and television. We take no rights stake as a condition of publishing, hold nothing for a term of years, and you never need our permission or involvement to license your work elsewhere.**

One point deserves separating clearly, because vetting checklists routinely blur it. True Haven Press holds the ISBN, which is *not* a rights instrument. An ISBN identifies the publisher of record—it is how our imprint appears on your sales listings and how the book is recognized as part of our catalog. Being the owner of the ISBNs assigned to your book versions gives us no claim on your work, no share of your sales, and no say in what you do with the book. Your rights live in your contract, and your contract leaves every one of them with you.

### How do royalties work, and how do I get paid?

**You keep 100% of your royalties, paid directly to you. Your book publishes under your own KDP and IngramSpark accounts, which means retailer payments land in your bank account rather than passing through ours. We take no percentage of your sales ever. There is no royalty statement to wait on from us.**

This is the mechanic that separates publishers most sharply, and it is worth asking everyone about in specific terms. Whose account is the book loaded under? Who receives money from the retailer first? How often is it passed along, and what comes out along the way? An author who does not ask can end up locked into an agreement where royalties arrive quarterly, minus a percentage, on a statement they have no way to audit.

Because the accounts are yours, you see your own sales dashboards in real time and you are never dependent on us for a number. Direct-sale links are also available to you, with better pricing and higher margins than any retail channel—useful for events, back-of-room sales, and your own audience—and always set up in your account, paid directly to you.

### Do I have to buy copies of my own book?

**No. You have unlimited access to author copies at cost for events, reviewers, and giveaways, plus direct-sell purchase links that carry better pricing and higher margins for you than any retail channel, all directly available in your KDP and IngramSpark accounts.**

Required bulk purchases are one of the defining mechanics of a vanity press. A contract obligating you to buy several hundred or several thousand copies has quietly moved the publisher's revenue from readers to you, which means they have already been paid whether or not the book ever sells. Read for that clause specifically, in any contract you are handed.

Another deceptive practice we see from vanity presses is including X number of books in the package price. It speaks to the author’s desire to have copies on hand as if this has much more value than it does—buying your own books from your own accounts is available to you 24/7 and at cost plus shipping, which is typically less than $10 per paperback.

### What happens if we part ways before the book is finished?

**Anything you have paid for is yours. Completed work and purchased assets—edited files, cover and interior design, anything already delivered—belong to you, and you leave with them. What does not transfer is our branding. The True Haven Press imprint and the ISBN remain our property, so you would publish without an imprint or with whoever finishes the project with you.**

Stated plainly, because it is better heard now than discovered later: the imprint and the ISBN travel together and both stay with us. The edition you go on to publish will need a new ISBN of its own. That is a small administrative step and a modest cost, not a lost book, and it is the only practical consequence of parting ways.

There is no clause in our agreement that makes leaving expensive, and nothing we hold back to make it difficult. We would rather part cleanly than keep an author who wants out, so upon request, we will update copyright pages and covers to remove ISBNs and imprint materials so you won’t have to pay someone else to do that, can work forward with what you’ve already purchased.

### How is hybrid publishing different from a vanity press?

**Selectivity, and where the money comes from. A vanity press accepts nearly everyone, because the author is the customer and the package is the product. A legitimate hybrid publisher declines manuscripts, puts its own name on the catalog, and has a genuine stake in whether your book turns out well.**

The label alone tells you nothing. Vanity presses adopted the word "hybrid" precisely because it sounds legitimate, which means you have to evaluate mechanics rather than marketing. The Independent Book Publishers Association maintains published Hybrid Publisher Criteria for exactly this purpose, and it is a fair standard to hold us to.

Ask any publisher you are considering, including us:

1.  What percentage of submissions do you accept? — About 40% of manuscripts submitted.

2.  Which rights do you take, and for how long? — None, for no term. You keep all of them.

3.  Whose retail accounts is the book published under? — Yours. Royalties go straight to you, and we take no percentage.

4.  Is there a minimum purchase requirement? — No, and author copies are available at cost plus shipping, directly in your accounts with KDP and IngramSpark.

5.  Can I see your books and hear from your authors? — Yes. Our catalog is public, and most of our authors are easy to find through their own channels.

6.  What share of your revenue comes from author fees versus book sales? — All of our revenue comes from author fees, because we take none of your royalties. Any publisher who gives authors 100% of sales revenue will fail that test by design, and we would rather say so than dress up the number.

> So test us on what that question is actually reaching for, which is whether we have a reason to care that your book is good. Our reason to care is the track record every client and every book carrying our imprint contributes to. Our name goes on the spine, our catalog is public, and we decline ~60% of what we are sent to keep it worth something. We also encourage our clients to write reviews and heavily rely on those truths about their experience working with us for future business.

## Section 3 — Process and production

### What kind of editing will my book get?

**Every book receives a full editorial pass from The Pro Book Editor, our in-house team—developmental editing, line editing, copy editing, and proofreading, in whatever combination your manuscript needs. The plan comes out of your assessment rather than a package tier, and you review and approve at every stage before anything moves forward.**

We do not cap revision rounds. You get as many passes as the book needs within the agreed scope, because a number written into a contract has a way of becoming a ceiling at the exact moment a book needs one more look.

Scope does have an edge, and it is fair to name it. It ends where revision stops being about the manuscript, where feedback is being relitigated rather than considered, and the same ground gets covered again without the book moving. That is rare, and we will raise it directly and early if it happens. It is one of the reasons we evaluate coachability before making an offer rather than after.

Our house standards are The Chicago Manual of Style and Merriam-Webster dictionary, applied consistently across the catalog. Where your book has good reason to depart from them—creativity, voice, dialect, genre convention, a deliberate stylistic choice—we build a style sheet around your book and hold that line instead. The goal is never to sand your voice into house style.

### How long does the publishing process take?

**Between 60 days and six months from signed agreement to release. The range is wide because two things drive it: how much work the manuscript needs, and how quickly you turn around reviews and approvals. You receive a schedule with real dates at the start, not a promise of "soon."**

Sixty days is a clean manuscript needing only a final proofread, with an author who reviews promptly. Six months is a book needing developmental work or ghostwriting support, or an author with a demanding schedule outside the project. Both are normal, and we will tell you which one you are looking at before you sign.

The largest variable is not us. Every stage has an author review built into it, and a manuscript waiting on approval holds up everything behind it. Authors who stay responsive routinely finish ahead of estimate. At the same time, we never rush authors. You control the timeline where your availability affects it, and we balance speed and high quality for ASAP results when the ball is in our court.

We would rather move a date than release a book that is not ready. If something needs another pass, we will tell you why and reset the schedule with you rather than around you.

### Where will my book be sold?

**Your book is distributed through both KDP and IngramSpark, under your own accounts. That pairing is deliberate: KDP maximizes your Amazon presence, and IngramSpark makes the book available to most retailers worldwide and orderable by brick-and-mortar bookstores and libraries.**

An honest note about bookstores, because it is where authors are most often oversold. Ingram distribution makes your book available for booksellers and librarians to order. It does not put copies on a shelf. Shelf placement is earned through demand, local relationships, events, and returnability, and any publisher offering bookstore placement as a deliverable is describing something they do not control.

What we do control is making sure nothing on the production side is the reason a bookseller says no: correct trim size, sensible pricing, a returnable listing where appropriate, a professional cover and interior, and clean metadata.

Category and keyword development is part of production, not an add-on. Getting them wrong is one of the most common and most invisible reasons a competent book underperforms. We research and build both before interior design begins, so this essential metadata is ready when the book goes up for publication. Categories determine which bestseller lists your book is eligible for and which browse paths lead to it; keywords determine which searches surface it.

### What marketing do you do, and what am I responsible for?

**Every book we publish receives release and event announcements across our reader subscription site and our own social channels, ongoing monthly rotation rather than launch-week-only promotion, and a permanent listing in the True Haven Press Bookstore linked to your Amazon page. Additional paid marketing services are also available. You’re responsible for investing time and/or budget in ongoing marketing of your title—it’s a long game, with everchanging rules and buyer behaviors, and we keep up with the trends so we’ll be able to customize marketing plans with you from day one and forward over time.**

Included with publication:

-   New release and special event announcements featured on Books, Authors & Publishing 411, our reader subscription site, and its social profiles on X, Facebook, and Instagram

-   New release announcements, milestone and event announcements, and ongoing monthly rotation across True Haven Press social profiles on Instagram, BookTok, Facebook, and LinkedIn

-   Inclusion in the True Haven Press Bookstore, with links to your Amazon page

What that is, and what it is not. It is real, continuing promotion to an audience of readers, and the monthly rotation matters more than the announcement—most books disappear from view the week after launch, and yours will not. It is not a publicity campaign, and we are not going to call it one.

What sells books over time is you showing up to your own audience consistently, in your own voice, for longer than a launch window. No publisher can do that for you. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something, usually at a price that reflects how much you wanted to believe it.

A dedicated marketing services page is in development and will list what can be purchased separately.

### Do you offer ghostwriting?

**Yes. Scope and price come out of a conversation rather than a menu: we look at what you have already written, talk through what the finished book needs to be, and identify what exists only in your head along with the best way to capture it. Some authors arrive with half a draft, some with notes and a clear vision.**

The assessment covers three things—the written material that already exists, the goal for the finished book, and how you work best. Some authors think out loud and are best captured in recorded conversation. Others write in fragments that need shaping rather than replacing. The scope and the price follow from that, which is why we cannot quote ghostwriting off a per-word rate.

On publication, we would rather be direct than clever. When you commission ghostwriting, the evaluation has already happened—we have read your material, agreed on what the book will be, and taken the work on. Publication is the plan from that point, not a decision held over you.

What is not automatic is our name on it. We can withhold True Haven Press branding from any project, at any point, if the book moves away from the plan we agreed on, or if enough of our guidance is declined that the finished product no longer reflects industry best practices or our standard of excellence. You would still have your book and every word of it. It would simply publish without our imprint.

That is the honest shape of it: the gate is at the end rather than the front. We are willing to write a book with you before we know exactly how it will turn out. We are not willing to put our name on one that stopped being the book we agreed to make.

## Section 4 — Working with us

### Is the work done by humans or AI?

**Your book is edited by highly trained, experienced humans. Our editors do not use AI. The single exception is PerfectIt, a consistency checker our proofreaders run in addition to—never instead of—their own read-through. No generative AI writes, rewrites, or edits your manuscript at any stage.**

Precisely who uses what:

-   Editors. No AI. Developmental, line, and copy editing are done by people start to finish.

-   Proofreaders. A full human read-through, plus PerfectIt as a quality-assurance pass. PerfectIt is a consistency checker that flags inconsistent hyphenation, capitalization, abbreviation, and departures from your style sheet across an entire manuscript. It does not generate text and does not rewrite sentences. It catches what a human eye reliably misses across 90,000 words, which is why it runs alongside a proofreader rather than in place of one. They review what it shows them and then make decisions to enter suggestions or not based on the Chicago Manual of Style, Merriam-Webster dictionary, and any style notes you’ve provided.

-   Managing editors and marketing specialists. We use Claude for research and for developing categories, keywords, and marketing content. Every result is reviewed, verified, and finalized by a human expert before it is used anywhere.

Both tools run on company-held paid accounts configured so that nothing uploaded or referenced can be used for AI training or shared beyond that account. Your manuscript is not training data, and it does not leave our control.

Here is the line that matters: your prose is never handed to a generative model and returned to you as editorial work.

If you used AI in your own drafting, tell us. That is your call to make, not ours to police, but it affects how the book gets listed. Amazon’s KDP policy distinguishes AI-generated content, which must be disclosed at publication, from AI-assisted content such as brainstorming or grammar checks, which does not. Classifying it correctly protects your listing; getting it wrong can put it at risk.

### Can I see books you have published, or talk to your authors?

**Yes to both. Our catalog is on the site, and most of our authors can be found through social media by searching their name. Out of respect for everyone’s privacy, we do not give out anyone’s contact information.**

Keep in mind while reviewing books we’ve published that each one represents a collaboration that gives the author final approval and everyone has different tastes. While we are selective and do withhold our imprint in some cases, there will be a range of books you might like and dislike in our catalogue.

### Can I talk with someone before I submit?

**Yes! Consultations are free and run as long as they need to—typically 30 to 60 minutes for a first conversation, and available again whenever you want while we work together.**

We do not use limited-time offers, we do not follow up with pressure, and we will not flatter you into a decision. If a conversation with us ends in you choosing a different path, that is a good outcome for both of us.

Call 1-800-585-3690, book a consultation, or submit your manuscript when you are ready.
